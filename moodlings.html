Moodlings

I kept seeing the same gap: people could sense how they felt, but struggled to find the words that truly captured it.

Through my behavioral and counseling work, I saw how difficult it could be to move from experiencing an emotion to identifying and articulating it. Broad labels like “good,” “bad,” or “stressed” often captured the intensity of a feeling, but not what was actually happening underneath.

What if finding the right words could be the beginning of reflection?

I saw an opportunity to make emotional vocabulary easier to explore—without telling people how they should feel.

A single check-in wasn't enough. The product needed to connect moments over time.

I designed Moodlings around a simple loop: capture how you feel, add context to the moment, and turn individual check-ins into patterns over time.

The experience needed to make reflection feel lightweight, not like another task.

I designed the check-in as a gradual flow—from sensing a broad feeling, to naming it more precisely, adding context, and reflecting only when users wanted to go deeper.

Make emotional vocabulary something users can build over time.

Each emotion becomes a collectible card users can unlock, revisit, and learn from—turning individual discoveries into a growing emotional vocabulary.

COLLECT → EXPLORE → LEARN

A single check-in captures a moment. Patterns emerge over time.

At the end of each month, users reflect on the month as a whole. These ratings build into long-term trends, while six-month and yearly summaries help users step back and see how their emotional patterns change over time.

Designed to move with users into therapy.

When needed, users can select a time range and choose what to include in a PDF report to bring into counseling — keeping control over what they share.

From concept to a working iOS product.

I built Moodlings in SwiftUI, translating the product and interaction design into a functional app and iterating directly through implementation.

PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

I scoped the first version around the core check-in loop, broke the work into smaller feature steps, and adjusted interactions as implementation exposed new constraints. Moving between research, design, and development helped me surface risks early and keep the product focused on the original user need.

Built with SwiftUI · Firebase · Google Sign-In

WHAT I LEARNED

Building the product showed me that implementation is part of product discovery. Technical constraints helped clarify priorities, expose incomplete interaction decisions, and shape a more focused experience.

NEXT DIRECTION

A future direction is to explore privacy-conscious AI-assisted emotion suggestions based on journal context. The system would offer vocabulary for users to consider while preserving user control and avoiding automated conclusions about how someone feels.
